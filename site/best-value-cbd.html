TL;DR: How to Find the Best Value CBD

Value = price ÷ total milligrams of CBD. Most retail tinctures cost $0.03–$0.12 per mg of CBD. Anything above ~$0.17/mg is widely graded as pricey; under ~$0.08/mg counts as bargain tier. Why Cost Per Milligram Is the Only Comparison That's Fair

A "$60 bottle" tells you nothing: one $60 bottle may hold 500mg while another holds 5,000mg. Divide price by total CBD mg and every product becomes apples-to-apples:

  • $40 ÷ 500mg = $0.080/mg — looks cheap, mid-tier value
  • $120 ÷ 1,000mg = $0.120/mg — premium pricing
  • $127 ÷ 10,000mg = $0.0127/mg — concentrate-only pricing

Why Our Number Is So Low (Honest Version)

We're not cheaper because we cut quality. We're cheaper because we removed three line items from the bottle:

  1. Carrier oil. A premixed tincture is mostly MCT or hemp seed oil — an ingredient you can buy at any grocery store for around $8. You pay CBD-extract prices for salad-oil weight.
  2. Premixing, bottling, and boxing. Glass, droppers, labels, cartons cost roughly the same whether the bottle is weak or strong — those fixed costs land on fewer milligrams in small bottles.
  3. Customer-acquisition ads. DTC CBD brands routinely spend $80–$120 per order acquiring customers. That spend lives inside the sticker price.

How to Verify Any Brand's Value Claim in 30 Seconds

  1. Find total CBD in mg on the label (not hemp extract, not hemp oil).
  2. Divide the price by that number.
  3. Compare against the bands: under $0.077/mg is bargain, $0.077–$0.167 is market, above $0.167 is pricey.
  4. If a batch certificate of analysis (CoA) is available, redo the division using its measured Total CBD figure.
